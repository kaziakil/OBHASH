# OBHASH Assessment Rubrics Specification

**Phase:** 3 of 8  
**Scope:** Complete 4-Level Rubrics for all 15 Assessment Dimensions  

---

## 1. Critical Thinking Rubrics

### Dimension 1: Evidence Evaluation
- **Criteria**: Distinction between correlation and causation; identification of voluntary selection bias; detection of unstated assumptions.
- **Level 1 (Emerging)**: Accepts claims without verifying sample validity; confuses correlation with causation.
- **Level 2 (Developing)**: Identifies general bias (e.g. manager preference) but misses structural sampling errors.
- **Level 3 (Demonstrating)**: Explicitly separates patent volume from innovation value; identifies voluntary survey selection bias.
- **Level 4 (Strongly Demonstrating)**: Rigorously deconstructs confounding variables (department mix, baseline differences) and critiques disproportionate policy overreach.

### Dimension 2: Perspective Evaluation
- **Criteria**: Recognition of institutional incentives; scrutiny of statistical framing; identification of safety vs efficiency trade-offs.
- **Level 1 (Emerging)**: Polarized view; accepts one source unconditionally while rejecting the other without evidence.
- **Level 2 (Developing)**: Recognizes differing views exist but treats them as irreconcilable without examining statistical base rates.
- **Level 3 (Demonstrating)**: Systematically compares metrics (percentages vs absolute figures); proposes empirical verification needed.
- **Level 4 (Strongly Demonstrating)**: Deconstructs institutional framing; recommends targeted policy experiments (geo-fencing, ADA corridor enforcement).

### Dimension 3: Judgment Under Uncertainty
- **Criteria**: Clear articulation of trade-offs (depth vs reach); risk mitigation; logical consistency.
- **Level 1 (Emerging)**: Impulsive choice without acknowledging significant drawbacks.
- **Level 2 (Developing)**: Identifies basic trade-offs but provides shallow defense.
- **Level 3 (Demonstrating)**: Defensible, quantified trade-off analysis with realistic mitigation strategies.
- **Level 4 (Strongly Demonstrating)**: Strategic synthesis analyzing second-order compounding effects and milestone gating.

## 2. Creative Problem-Solving Rubrics

### Dimension 1: Originality & Idea Generation
- **Criteria**: Conceptual diversity across domains; non-obviousness; feasibility awareness.
- **Level 1 (Emerging)**: Cliché solutions (e.g. "download another reminder app").
- **Level 2 (Developing)**: Multiple ideas, but all remain derivative of standard apps.
- **Level 3 (Demonstrating)**: Ideas span hardware, behavioral nudges, and cooperative loops; details a viable creative mechanism.
- **Level 4 (Strongly Demonstrating)**: Elegant paradigm reframe; addresses social friction and builds self-correcting incentives.

### Dimension 2: Practical Reasoning Under Constraints
- **Criteria**: Strict adherence to zero-power, $100 budget, 4km scale constraints; resourceful repurposing of materials.
- **Level 1 (Emerging)**: Violates core constraints (assumes cellular networks or generators work).
- **Level 2 (Developing)**: Complies with constraints but proposes brittle, unscalable ideas (one person walking).
- **Level 3 (Demonstrating)**: Structured mesh communication using physical relays, timestamped bulletin boards, and bicycle dispatchers.
- **Level 4 (Strongly Demonstrating)**: Resilient human mesh protocol with message authentication, return confirmations, and incentive alignment.

### Dimension 3: Iterative Thinking & Solution Improvement
- **Criteria**: Root psychological diagnosis; low-friction micro-collaborations; anticipation of unintended consequences.
- **Level 1 (Emerging)**: Superficial changes ("send more emails" or "give gift cards").
- **Level 2 (Developing)**: Diagnoses awkwardness but introduces heavy administrative reporting burden.
- **Level 3 (Demonstrating)**: Replaces open-ended meetings with concrete shared micro-challenges; mitigates mentor fatigue.
- **Level 4 (Strongly Demonstrating)**: Structural overhaul creating reciprocal incentives and gradual escalation from async tasks to high-trust meetups.

## 3. Curiosity Rubrics

### Dimension 1: Question Formulation
- **Criteria**: Depth and diversity of questions; distinction between descriptive and mechanistic inquiries; falsifiable hypotheses.
- **Level 1 (Emerging)**: Basic descriptive questions ("what color is it?").
- **Level 2 (Developing)**: Relevant questions staying within familiar templates without probing the core anomaly.
- **Level 3 (Demonstrating)**: Multi-layered mechanistic questions probing environmental synchronization cues and biological clocks.
- **Level 4 (Strongly Demonstrating)**: Profound cross-disciplinary inquiries; designs an elegant variable-isolation experiment.

### Dimension 2: Intellectual Openness & Claim Investigation
- **Criteria**: Avoidance of credulity and cynicism; clinical trial standards; falsifiability definition.
- **Level 1 (Emerging)**: Gullibly cites popularity or reflexively dismisses claim without investigation.
- **Level 2 (Developing)**: Notes red flags but offers generic skepticism ("I'd Google it").
- **Level 3 (Demonstrating)**: Demands randomized sham-controlled double-blind trials; articulates balanced falsification criteria.
- **Level 4 (Strongly Demonstrating)**: Explores underlying neuroscience (40Hz gamma oscillations) while maintaining rigorous epistemic humility.

### Dimension 3: Cross-Disciplinary Exploration
- **Criteria**: Depth of structural parallels; algorithmic insight over superficial metaphors; novel application proposal.
- **Level 1 (Emerging)**: Superficial poetic remarks ("both are like webs").
- **Level 2 (Developing)**: Identifies basic network similarities without generating new insight.
- **Level 3 (Demonstrating)**: Substantive mechanistic parallels (self-healing, chemical gradient routing vs packet delivery).
- **Level 4 (Strongly Demonstrating)**: Synthesizes ecological resilience with Byzantine fault tolerance and Sybil defense in decentralized computing.
